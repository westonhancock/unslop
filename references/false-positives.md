# False positives and human signals

Load this before any detect pass or aggressive edit. The most common failure of an AI-cleanup skill is gutting legitimate prose because it matched a keyword.

## Not evidence of AI

A clean human writer hits several patterns from the skill without any AI involvement. None of the following is a reliable indicator on its own.

- **Perfect grammar and consistent style.** Many writers are professionals or have been edited. Polish is not AI.
- **Mixed casual and formal registers.** Usually signals a person in a technical field, a young writer, or someone with neurodivergent prose habits, not a chatbot.
- **Bland or robotic prose.** AI prose has *specific* tells. Generic dryness without them is just dry writing.
- **Formal or academic vocabulary.** AI overuses a specific set of fancy words, not all fancy words. Don't flatten "ostensibly" or "constituent" because they sound brainy.
- **Common transition words in isolation.** *Additionally*, *moreover*, *consequently* are AI-coded only when piled up. One *however* is not a tell.
- **Curly quotes alone.** macOS, Word, Google Docs, and most CMSes auto-curl by default.
- **Em dashes alone.** Many editors and journalists use them constantly. Em dashes are evidence only alongside formulaic, sales-y rhythm.
- **One short emphatic sentence.** Humans use clipped sentences to land a point. Flag staccato drama only when several fragments run together and inflate the tone.
- **"Honestly" or "look" mid-sentence.** Ordinary in casual writing. The tell is the standalone theatrical opener.
- **A letter-style opening or closing.** Salutations and sign-offs predate ChatGPT by centuries.
- **Unsourced claims.** Most writing is unsourced. Missing citations prove nothing.
- **Correct, complex formatting.** Visual editors and templates produce clean output with no AI involved.
- **Secondhand text.** Never rewrite a watched phrase inside a quotation, title, proper name, code block, or an example where the phrase is being *discussed* rather than used.

**Rule: look for clusters, not isolates.** A single em dash means nothing. Em dashes plus a rule-of-three plus "vibrant tapestry" plus a Conclusion section is a confession.

## Signs of human writing — preserve these

When you see these, lean toward leaving the prose alone. They are evidence of a real person, and over-editing destroys exactly what makes the piece sound human.

- **Specific, unusual, hard-to-fabricate detail.** A real address. A weird quote. "The lawyer who used to work upstairs from my dentist." Models round off specifics; humans hoard them.
- **Mixed feelings and unresolved tension.** "I think this is mostly good, but it bothers me and I can't fully explain why." Models default to clean takes.
- **A mind visibly changing on the page.** The last third knowing something the first third didn't.
- **Dated, era-bound references.** Slang, memes, or in-jokes that map to a specific year and subculture. Models lag by a year or more.
- **First-person editorial choices the writer can defend.** If they can explain why they made a cut or chose a word, that's a strong human signal.
- **Genuine asymmetry.** A long digression, a one-word paragraph, an aside, wildly uneven section lengths. Uniformity is the machine signature.
- **Variety in sentence length.** Real writing alternates unevenly. AI writing tends toward a steady mid-length cadence.
- **Self-corrections and parentheticals.** "(I keep wanting to say 'almost' here, but it really was certain.)" Models rarely interrupt themselves.
- **Anything written before November 30, 2022.** ChatGPT's public launch. With rare exceptions, older text is not AI-written.

## The critical caveat

Read this list as **"preserve these when they are true,"** never as **"add these."**

An invented confession, a decorative hedge, a plausible-sounding number, or a manufactured one-word paragraph is a fabrication, and it is also the next-generation tell. Planted humanity reads as fake within a paragraph. These traits get earned in the reporting and the thinking, or they stay out.

## Over-scrub check

After an aggressive edit, ask whether the result reads like *this writer* or like *a de-slopped draft*. De-slopping has its own texture: uniformly clear, uniformly direct, every paragraph the same tidy weight, a short punchy sentence alternating with a long one on a schedule. That texture is detectable too. If the edit produced it, put some unevenness back by restoring the writer's original lines, not by inventing new quirks.
